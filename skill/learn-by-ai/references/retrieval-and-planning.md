# Retrieval and lesson planning

Use this workflow before teaching a new node, after a prerequisite detour changes the node, or before a substantial reteach. Reuse a still-current source pack for ordinary follow-up turns.

## Frame the unit

Define a provisional lesson frame from the goal, graph, learner state, and available time:

- one observable outcome;
- prerequisite assumptions that matter now;
- the smallest coherent scope that can reach the outcome;
- adjacent material explicitly deferred;
- evidence that will count as completion;
- an approximate time or turn budget and a natural stopping point.

The frame guides retrieval and may change after sources or learner evidence reveal a real dependency.

## Search the master corpus first

Treat the resources already indexed for the learning project as the master corpus. Search the index, tables of contents, headings, keywords, synonyms, notation variants, and known prerequisite terms before opening large documents. Extract only what the unit needs:

- definitions, assumptions, and notation;
- the central argument, mechanism, derivation, or proof structure;
- one canonical example and any important boundary case;
- source-specific sequencing or method conventions;
- precise locators and any conflict among sources.

Keep concise original notes and locators; do not copy whole chapters or build a full-text mirror. When a locator cannot be verified, record topic-level coverage and the uncertainty.

## Add targeted external research

Use available web or literature-search tools to fill named gaps in the corpus. Prefer original papers, official documentation, standards, or authoritative institutional material for claims and conditions. Use reputable reviews or textbooks for synthesis, and strong technical or educational blogs for intuition, worked examples, implementation details, or alternative explanations. For time-sensitive topics, prioritize current sources and record dates.

Search with a purpose such as resolving a conflict, checking currency, finding a clearer mechanism, locating an application, or designing a transfer task. Record the title, URL or identifier, access date, relevant locator, source role, and the claim or teaching move it supports. Cross-check consequential or disputed claims; present unresolved disagreements explicitly. Never place private learner text, unpublished material, credentials, or sensitive project details into an external query.

If external search is unavailable, state the research gap and proceed from the master corpus when the unit remains reliable. Pause only when current external evidence is essential to the promised outcome.

## Stop retrieval and freeze the source pack

A source pack is sufficient when it has:

- an accessible anchor for the unit's definitions and conceptual order;
- adequate support for each consequential planned claim and condition;
- enough material for a complete example, a contrast or boundary, and an assessment;
- no unresolved conflict that would materially change the lesson.

Most units need one anchor and a few complementary sources, with the exact number driven by uncertainty and stakes. Stop when another search is unlikely to change the explanation, route, or assessment. Expand retrieval only for a named gap; discovery of an interesting adjacent topic goes to the backlog.

## Freeze a flexible lesson contract

Before teaching, summarize:

```text
Outcome:
Prerequisites assumed or checked:
Included scope / deferred scope:
Source pack and role of each source:
Conceptual arc:
Teach -> guided derivation -> independent transfer:
Completion evidence:
Approximate budget:
Detour and stopping rule:
```

Treat this as a working contract. Preserve the outcome and completion evidence unless new learner evidence makes them inappropriate; adapt pacing, examples, representation, and the shortest prerequisite path as needed.

## Prevent loops and uncontrolled expansion

- Keep one active learning objective and one active prerequisite detour. If the detour grows into a separate unit, checkpoint it and return or stop at the natural boundary.
- Every teaching cycle must produce new observable evidence, repair a named breakpoint, or complete a planned conceptual step. Repetition without state change triggers a different representation, a smaller task, or a stop.
- After two failures at the same breakpoint, follow the remediation rule instead of extending the explanation or adding sources.
- Do not add concepts merely because research found them. Defer them unless they are required for the stated outcome.
- Stop the unit when completion evidence is obtained, the agreed capacity is reached at a natural boundary, or remaining work needs a distinct prerequisite unit.
- Record unfinished work, source gaps, and valuable deferred topics in the checkpoint so the next session can resume without reopening broad research.
